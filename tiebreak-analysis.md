## Item 3 — MDL 3172, `b2a_timing`

Sources: CourtListener [ECF 5](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72249116/5/in-re-cartiva-synthetic-cartilage-implant-products-liability-litigation/) and [ECF 15](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72249116/15/in-re-cartiva-synthetic-cartilage-implant-products-liability-litigation/), read together.

### Provision or provisions in play

The Order Upon Transfer says:

> “Prior to the initial conference, counsel for plaintiffs and counsel for defendants shall, to the extent they have not already done so, confer and seek consensus on the selection of a candidate for the position of liaison counsel for each group who will serve as the primary contact person for the Court regarding administrative matters.” (ECF 5 ¶ 7.)

The chambers letter sets the hearing for August 26, 2026, invites organizational proposals, and says:

> “The deadline for submitting such proposals is Wednesday, August 19, 2026, and these proposals should be submitted only by email to KGBchambers@ared.uscourts.gov.” (ECF 15 at 2.)

### `reached`

- **Case for TRUE:** “Prior to the initial conference” and the August 19 proposal deadline put an express timetable around the process that may culminate in leadership appointments; on a functional reading, the timing question is therefore within the initial management process.
- **Case for FALSE:** The subject is the timing **of appointments**, while these provisions time counsel's conferral, candidate selection, and proposals; neither document requires the parties to address when the court should make an appointment.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** The codebook separately identifies appointment timing and selection procedure, and the quoted deadlines govern the latter.

### `express`

- **Case for TRUE:** The order's own words connect a timing marker (“Prior to the initial conference”) to “selection of a candidate,” so a reader can see a leadership timetable without consulting Rule 16.1.
- **Case for FALSE:** No language names or describes when an appointment should occur; timing a precursor is not describing the timing of the appointment itself.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** The text expressly describes selection timing, not appointment timing.

### `party_direction`

- **Case for TRUE:** Counsel “shall . . . confer and seek consensus” before the conference and must submit any organizational proposals by August 19, which are mandatory or conditional acts on a leadership timetable.
- **Case for FALSE:** Those acts concern candidate selection and organizational proposals, not a position or act concerning when the court should appoint leadership.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** The subject-specific direction falls under selection procedure or structure rather than `b2a_timing`.

### `court_resolution`

- **Case for TRUE:** The court fixed the preconference conferral period, proposal deadline, and hearing date, so the parties cannot change that sequence through a Rule 16.1 report alone.
- **Case for FALSE:** The court did not fix when any appointment will be made; it said it would consider proposals “to make these determinations,” leaving appointment timing open.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** No operative appointment date or appointment-timing rule is set.

**Confidence: medium.** The ambiguity is whether a deadline for candidate selection and organizational proposals counts as timing “of the appointments” or belongs exclusively to the separately coded selection procedure.

## Item 22 — MDL 3172, `b3f_magistrate_master`

Sources: CourtListener [ECF 5](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72249116/5/in-re-cartiva-synthetic-cartilage-implant-products-liability-litigation/) and [ECF 15](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72249116/15/in-re-cartiva-synthetic-cartilage-implant-products-liability-litigation/), read together.

### Provision or provisions in play

The chambers letter says:

> “United States Magistrate Judge Patricia S. Harris may join our call and is assigned to this litigation with me.” (ECF 15 at 3.)

### `reached`

- **Case for TRUE:** An identified magistrate judge “is assigned to this litigation,” which is an operative treatment of magistrate-judge involvement and not merely an external Rule reference.
- **Case for FALSE:** Rule 16.1(b)(3)(F) concerns whether **matters** should be referred; a routine case assignment and possible attendance on a call do not put referral of any matter into play.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** “Assigned to this litigation with me” is best read as bringing magistrate-judge involvement within the order's operative scope.

### `express`

- **Case for TRUE:** The order identifies the magistrate judge by name and expressly states her assignment, so a reader need not consult Rule 16.1.
- **Case for FALSE:** The text never uses “refer,” identifies no referred matter, and says only that she “may join” a call; a magistrate judge's name alone need not describe the coded subject.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** The assignment language describes more than the magistrate judge's mere presence on the court's staff.

### `party_direction`

- **Not contestable. Recommendation: FALSE.** No provision asks the parties to propose, address, or act on a magistrate referral.

### `court_resolution`

- **Case for TRUE:** The letter states that Judge Harris “is assigned to this litigation,” a present operative determination the parties cannot alter through a report.
- **Case for FALSE:** Assignment to a docket is not necessarily a referral under Rule 72 or 28 U.S.C. § 636, and the letter fixes no category of matters for the magistrate judge to decide or supervise.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** Under the codebook's partial-resolution rule, the court has at least fixed who the assigned magistrate judge is, even though her functions remain open.

**Confidence: medium.** The genuine ambiguity is whether “assigned to this litigation with me” is itself a referral determination or only an administrative assignment preceding any referral of a matter.

## Item 37 — MDL 3163, `b3b_factual_basis_exchange`

Source: [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/72052106/in-re-glp-1-ras-naion-products-liability-litigation/), ECF 2.

### Provision or provisions in play

The order requires:

> “Plaintiffs and Defendants shall submit to the Court by email . . . a brief written statement indicating their preliminary understanding of the facts involved in the litigation and the critical factual and legal issues.” (§ III.)

Its anticipated conference agenda includes:

> “Desirability, timing, and requirements for Plaintiff Fact Sheets” (§ IV(B)(9).)

The order then says:

> “The foregoing is not an exhaustive list of the matters that will be addressed at the January conference. Counsel are encouraged to meet and confer with each other in anticipation of the conference.” (ECF 2 at 5.)

### `reached`

- **Case for TRUE:** Plaintiff fact sheets are a recognizable mechanism for exchanging claim-specific factual information, and the order says their “desirability, timing, and requirements” will be addressed at the conference.
- **Case for FALSE:** The required statements go only “to the Court,” not between the parties, and the fact-sheet item merely forecasts a court discussion rather than requiring the parties to address how and when they will exchange factual bases.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** The express fact-sheet agenda item is enough to bring the exchange mechanism and its timing within the initial management process.

### `express`

- **Case for TRUE:** “Plaintiff Fact Sheets” together with their “timing” and “requirements” describes a factual-information exchange mechanism without requiring consultation of Rule 16.1.
- **Case for FALSE:** The order never says that fact sheets will be exchanged, what facts they will contain, or that they concern the factual bases for claims and defenses; a reader could view the label as an undefined future topic.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** The words “Plaintiff Fact Sheets,” especially when paired with “timing” and “requirements,” sufficiently describe a factual-information exchange mechanism.

### `party_direction`

- **Case for TRUE:** All parties' counsel must attend the conference, the court says the listed matters “will be addressed,” and counsel are “encouraged to meet and confer” beforehand; the separate mandatory factual statements require both sides to articulate their understanding of the facts.
- **Case for FALSE:** No sentence tells the parties to propose, submit, confer about, or otherwise act specifically on a fact-sheet exchange; “the Court anticipates discussing” and a general encouragement to confer are weaker than the codebook's subject-specific direction.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** The order identifies a conference topic but does not expressly assign the parties a subject-specific act concerning the exchange.

### `court_resolution`

- **Case for TRUE:** The court fixed deadlines and a form for each side's factual position statement and fixed that fact sheets will be a conference topic, none of which the parties can alter through a later report.
- **Case for FALSE:** Statements emailed to the court are not a party-to-party exchange, and no fact sheet is ordered, timed, required, or defined; the operative exchange treatment remains open.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** The court has scheduled deliberation about fact sheets, not fixed any part of the factual-basis exchange itself.

**Confidence: medium.** The ambiguity is whether naming plaintiff fact sheets as a future conference topic both expressly reaches the Rule 16.1 subject and directs party action, despite the absence of an ordered exchange or proposal.

## Item 41 — MDL 3181, `b2d_direct_filing`

Sources: [CourtListener record](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/73502651/2/in-re-boston-scientific-spinal-cord-stimulator-products-liability-litigation/) and the [official court copy of Pretrial Order No. 1](https://www.cacd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2_Pretrial-Order-No.-1.pdf).

### Provision or provisions in play

The preliminary report must include:

> “An estimate of the total number of related cases counsel expect to be filed. A statement of counsel's view(s) on best practices for docket management in light of the estimated total number of cases (i.e., whether documents affecting all actions be filed only on the MDL docket, or also on the dockets of the individual actions).” (§ 5(b)(iii).)

The interim filing provisions say:

> “All documents relating to any cases in this MDL action shall be filed in this Master file.” (§ 6(b)(ii).)

And:

> “Later Filed Cases. This Order shall also apply to related cases later filed in, removed to, or transferred to this Court.” (§ 6(f).)

### `reached`

- **Case for TRUE:** “Related cases later filed in . . . this Court” describes direct-filed new actions, and the order makes its master-docket regime applicable to them.
- **Case for FALSE:** The order never asks whether direct filing should be permitted or how a plaintiff initiates a new MDL action; “later filed” is a general scope clause that can include ordinary local cases later related or transferred.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** Direct-filed cases are at least an express subset of cases “later filed in . . . this Court,” and the order prescribes some treatment for them.

### `express`

- **Case for TRUE:** A reader can see from “related cases later filed in . . . this Court” that new filings in the transferee court are in play without consulting Rule 16.1.
- **Case for FALSE:** “Direct filing” is a term of art involving permission and procedures for commencing an action directly on the MDL docket; the order does not name or describe those mechanics.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** The codebook permits description rather than talismanic wording, and “later filed in . . . this Court” describes the relevant category.

### `party_direction`

- **Case for TRUE:** Because the order applies to later-filed cases, its command that “[a]ll documents” be filed in the master file tells counsel how filings associated with those cases must be handled; the report also requires estimates and docket-management views about future cases.
- **Case for FALSE:** None of those commands tells a plaintiff how to file the **new action itself**; the master-file direction governs documents after a case belongs to the MDL, and the report concerns general docket management.
- **Recommendation: FALSE.** The order contains no subject-specific act for commencing a directly filed new action.

### `court_resolution`

- **Case for TRUE:** The court decided that its order applies to related cases later filed in the district and that documents for MDL cases go in the master file, fixing at least part of how direct-filed cases will be managed.
- **Case for FALSE:** A scope rule after filing is not a direct-filing protocol; the parties remain free to propose permission, venue allegations, fees, service, tagging, and other initiation mechanics in their report.
- **Recommendation: TRUE.** Under partial resolution, applying the order and master-docket rule to later-filed related cases is an operative determination even though most direct-filing mechanics remain open.

**Confidence: medium.** The ambiguity is whether a general later-filed-case scope and master-docket rule count as treatment of direct filing or only treatment after an action has independently entered the MDL.
