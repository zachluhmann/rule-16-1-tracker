Why this exists

On the day Rule 16.1 took effect, Crowell & Moring told clients that "it will likely be several years before the impact of Rule 16.1 is clear." Eleven weeks later Sidley Austin reported that "[s]ince December 1, 2025, courts have taken varied approaches" — describing the variation accurately, and naming no court and counting none.

Both are right that the variation is the story. Neither says how much. To my knowledge nobody has assembled the population and counted it, so this does — every MDL the Judicial Panel has centralized since the effective date, every enumerated topic in the Rule, coded from the order text with a pin cite.

Sources: Crowell & Moring, FRCP 16.1 Now in Play for MDLs—Game Changer or Not? (Dec. 1, 2025); Sidley Austin, New Year, New Rule: A Fresh Framework for Multidistrict Litigation (Feb. 19, 2026). Both quotations verified against the source, 11 Aug 2026.

The headline reversed once already. The first three orders coded here all cited the Rule — a 100% uptake rate. That was an artifact: those three surfaced from a full-text search for the phrase, and a full-text search can only find documents that use the phrase. Enumerating the JPML's universe and reading every docket moved it to 50%. Any measure of this Rule's uptake built from full-text search will substantially overstate it.

How to read this

Three coding rules govern, and they are conservative by design. They are the whole product.

  • Code only what a document says — never what it appears to mean. Not a party's characterization of an order, not a firm alert describing it, not a docket-entry summary.
  • Silence is NOT_ADDRESSED, never NO. Only an affirmative refusal is NO. That distinction is the most common coding error and the one most likely to get a dataset attacked.
  • Undercount when unsure. If it is not clear whether something qualifies, it doesn't. An undercount is a floor a court can rely on. An overcount is a number a court will not touch. Every figure here is a floor.

Ambiguity is coded UNCLEAR with the language quoted rather than resolved. Sources link to free, stable CourtListener documents or to the transferee court's own published copy — never to PACER.

Limitations, stated openly

A stated limitation is a credibility asset. A quiet gap is what gets a dataset dismissed.

  • Coverage is 14 of 16. One order exists and has not been read: MDL 3187, where four orders sit on the docket and none carries a text layer. One MDL — 3176 — has issued no qualifying order at all, which is a finding about that proceeding, not a gap. Those two categories are never merged. The block rate reported here is 1 of 16 (6%), defined as MDLs whose existing order could not be read.
  • The pin cites are two-tiered. Six rows (3162, 3163, 3166, 3171, 3174, 3175) carry paragraph pointers without verbatim quoted language; the other eight carry full quotations captured at read time. The thin six were coded earliest, before the discipline tightened. They are being back-filled. Four of the seven citing orders sit in that thin tier, so the headline rests partly on rows that do not yet meet the standard the later rows set.
  • Initial orders only, with one exception. Later case-management orders may invoke the Rule where the first did not — one already does, in MDL 3175.
  • The report layer is two read, three known. Rule 16.1's only real mandates run to the parties' report, and two of fourteen courts route that report to a chambers email address rather than the docket — one instructing that it "should not be filed." Where the report never reaches the docket, no docket-based method, including this one, can observe it.
  • One coder, no second rater. There is no inter-coder reliability statistic. Every coding decision carries a pin cite so that disagreement is with a quotation rather than with a judgment.
  • N is small and the Rule is eight months old. Nothing here supports a claim about long-run practice.

Time from transfer to initial management conference

Rule 16.1(a) provides that the transferee court "should schedule an initial management conference" and sets no time. This is the cleanest measure in the dataset — one variable, no coding judgment, trivially reproducible.

A standing rule

This dataset is not a litigation resource. If a party asks that an entry be added, removed, or reframed to help a position, the request will be declined and the fact of it noted here. The neutrality is what makes the thing worth citing.

No judge's motives, competence, or compliance is characterized anywhere in this data. cites_rule = NO is a fact. "Ignored the rule" is not, and does not appear.
