Rule 16.1 is the first Federal Rule of Civil Procedure written specifically for multidistrict litigation. On the effective date, Crowell & Moring told clients that "it will likely be several years before the impact of Rule 16.1 is clear." Eleven weeks later, Sidley Austin reported that "[s]ince December 1, 2025, courts have taken varied approaches," identifying no court and giving no figure. I am aware of no published count of how transferee courts have applied the Rule. This is that count.

Crowell & Moring, FRCP 16.1 Now in Play for MDLs: Game Changer or Not? (Dec. 1, 2025); Sidley Austin, New Year, New Rule: A Fresh Framework for Multidistrict Litigation (Feb. 19, 2026). Both quotations verified against the source, 11 Aug 2026.

Findings

  1. Seven of the fourteen readable orders cite Rule 16.1 by name; seven do not. All fourteen order a report and all fourteen address leadership counsel, so the Rule is not being disregarded outright. But the citing orders reach further: they address roughly sixteen of the nineteen items tracked here, against roughly ten for the orders that do not cite it. Two non-citing orders designate the Manual for Complex Litigation instead. The more plausible reading is not that courts have ignored Rule 16.1, but that Rule 16.1 has not displaced the Manual.
  2. Leadership counsel is the only item every order addresses, 14 of 14, including all seven that never cite the Rule. The least-addressed items are direct filing and measures to facilitate resolution, at 6 of 14 each.
  3. No order has addressed disclosure of third-party litigation funding, 0 of 14. Rule 16.1 does not require the subject to be raised; subdivision (b)(4) leaves it to the parties. The null is the finding.
  4. The interval from transfer to the initial conference runs from 21 days to 198, a ninefold spread across the twelve orders that set a date. Rule 16.1(a) provides that the court "should schedule an initial management conference" and sets no deadline.
  5. Where the Rule is applied, it appears to travel as a form order. Judge Quraishi's Initial Procedure Order No. 1 in the Dupixent MDL (D.N.J., June 2026) is the same form order Judge Bates entered in D.D.C. five months earlier, including three added items that appear verbatim in both and in neither the Rule nor the Committee Note.
  6. Uptake measured by full-text search is biased upward. The first three orders examined here all cited the Rule, an apparent rate of 100%. Those three were located by searching for the phrase, and such a search returns only documents that use it. Enumerating the Panel's full list and reading every order produced 50%.
  7. The Rule's one mandatory provision has already been departed from. Rule 16.1(b)(1) contemplates that the parties meet and submit a report. In the Trans Union MDL, one plaintiff group filed its own Rule 16.1 report unilaterally, five weeks after the effective date.

Nineteen columns cover the Rule's eighteen listed items: this dataset splits (b)(2)(A)(iii) into selection of leadership and periodic review of it.

Rule 16.1

The Rule is short and largely permissive. The transferee court should hold an early conference and should order a report beforehand; what the report must contain is the Rule's only obligation, and it binds the parties rather than the court. The report must cover eighteen listed items: seven concerning leadership counsel, four on scheduling, direct filing and related actions, and seven on the parties' initial views. Anything else is permitted but not required, which is why litigation funding appears here as a null rather than as a violation.

Full text of Rule 16.1

(a) Initial Management Conference. After the Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation transfers actions, the transferee court should schedule an initial management conference to develop an initial plan for orderly pretrial activity in the MDL proceedings.

(b) Report for the Conference.

(1) Submitting a Report. The transferee court should order the parties to meet and to submit a report to the court before the conference.

(2) Required Content: the Parties' Views on Leadership Counsel and Other Matters. The report must address any matter the court designates—which may include any matter in Rule 16—and, unless the court orders otherwise, the parties' views on:

(A) whether leadership counsel should be appointed and, if so:

(i) the timing of the appointments;

(ii) the structure of leadership counsel;

(iii) the procedure for selecting leadership and whether the appointments should be reviewed periodically;

(iv) their responsibilities and authority in conducting pretrial activities and any role in facilitating resolution of the MDL proceedings;

(v) the proposed methods for regularly communicating with and reporting to the court and nonleadership counsel;

(vi) any limits on activity by nonleadership counsel; and

(vii) whether and when to establish a means for compensating leadership counsel;

(B) any previously entered scheduling or other orders that should be vacated or modified;

(C) a schedule for additional management conferences with the court;

(D) how to manage the direct filing of new actions in the MDL proceedings; and

(E) whether related actions have been—or are expected to—be filed in other courts, and whether to adopt methods for coordinating with them.

(3) Additional Required Content: the Parties' Initial Views on Various Matters. Unless the court orders otherwise, the report also must address the parties' initial views on:

(A) whether consolidated pleadings should be prepared;

(B) how and when the parties will exchange information about the factual bases for their claims and defenses;

(C) discovery, including any difficult issues that may arise;

(D) any likely pretrial motions;

(E) whether the court should consider any measures to facilitate resolving some or all actions before the court;

(F) whether any matters should be referred to a magistrate judge or a master; and

(G) the principal factual and legal issues likely to be presented.

(4) Permitted Content. The report may include any other matter that the parties wish to bring to the court's attention.

(c) Initial Management Order. After the conference, the court should enter an initial management order addressing the matters in Rule 16.1(b) and, in the court's discretion, any other matters. This order controls the course of the proceedings unless the court modifies it.

Quoted verbatim, including the Rule's own punctuation. Text as published by the Legal Information Institute, Cornell Law School, law.cornell.edu/rules/frcp/rule_16.1, retrieved 12 August 2026. Confirm against the official text before quoting the Rule in a filing.

Method

The universe is taken from the Judicial Panel's monthly list of pending MDLs rather than from a search, so it includes proceedings whose orders never use the phrase "Rule 16.1." For each MDL, the transferee court's first management order is retrieved and read in full, and every variable is coded from the order text with a pin cite to the paragraph. Three conventions govern:

  • Code the document, not descriptions of it. Not a party's characterization of an order, not a firm alert, not the docket entry.
  • Silence is recorded as not addressed, never as a refusal. Only an affirmative declination is coded as one.
  • Ambiguity is recorded as unclear, with the language quoted rather than resolved. Every figure here is a floor.

Sources link to free copies on CourtListener or on the court's own site; none requires a PACER account. Corrections to any coding decision are welcome and are recorded in the changelog.

Limitations

  • Coverage is 14 of 16. One order exists and has not been read: MDL 3187, whose four docketed orders carry no text layer. One MDL, 3176, has issued no qualifying order, which is a fact about that proceeding rather than a gap in the data. The two are never merged. The block rate is 1 of 16.
  • The pin cites are two-tiered. Six rows (3162, 3163, 3166, 3171, 3174, 3175) give paragraph pointers without quoted language; the other eight quote the language directly. The six were coded earliest, and four of the seven citing orders sit among them. They are being back-filled.
  • Initial orders only, with one exception: a later order may invoke the Rule where the first did not, as one already has in MDL 3175.
  • Reports routed to chambers are invisible. Two of the fourteen courts direct counsel to email the report rather than file it, one stating that it "should not be filed." No docket-based method, including this one, can observe those.
  • One coder, no second rater, and so no inter-rater statistic. Every entry carries a pin cite so that disagreement is with a quotation.
  • N is small and the Rule is eight months old. Nothing here supports a claim about settled practice.
